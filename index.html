A clock drawn
across the land.

Six fixed color bands show sunset by the local clock on the selected date: before 5 pm, 5–6 pm, 6–7 pm, 7–8 pm, 8–9 pm, and 9 pm or later. Tap any filled area for its modeled sunset time and UTC offset.

Why colors jump at borders

Time zones change the clock, not the Sun. A sunset of 7:40 pm on one side of a border can be 8:40 pm on the other, so the border is part of the edge between the 7–8 pm and 8–9 pm areas. The fill follows those edges as well as the solar curves. Daylight saving time creates jumps on March 8 and November 1. Most of Arizona stays on standard time; the Navajo Nation observes daylight saving, while Hopi does not.

A modeled horizon

We use Astronomy Engine and a solar-center altitude of −0.833333°, modeling standard refraction over an unobstructed sea-level horizon. Mountains, elevation, and weather can change the sunset you see. Solar curves are precomputed on a fine north–south grid (approximately 0.1° latitude). Bands are clipped to U.S. land and time zones; shared map boundaries are simplified at 0.002° for display.
